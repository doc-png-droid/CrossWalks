Method, Scope, and Sources

This is a leadership and curriculum-discussion tool. It identifies overlap in cognitive practices; it does not claim that SIS created AP practices or that College Board endorses the SIS framework.

Scoring method

ScoreMeaning
3Direct / defining alignment: the AP overview explicitly requires a close equivalent and makes it central to disciplinary performance.
2Clear supporting alignment: the equivalent practice appears explicitly but is not the defining center of the course.
1Indirect / enabling alignment: the habit plausibly supports success or is built into the course structure, but is not named as an AP skill outcome.
0Not evident in the public overview reviewed; this is not a claim that the habit is absent from SIS teaching or full AP materials.

Key findings

  • 20 / 20 habits have at least one defensible connection across SIS AP offerings.
  • 13 / 20 are directly mirrored by recurring AP skill language; 7 are mainly enabling learning behaviors or instructional conditions.
  • The broadest AP-wide convergence is around explaining with principles and evidence, applying knowledge, connecting ideas or representations, making meaning, and communicating reasoning. Inquiry, prediction, hypothesis testing, contrast, revision, and error analysis become especially strong in particular disciplines.

Scope decisions

SIS offering count
The SIS site states 24 AP offerings. AP Microeconomics and AP Macroeconomics are shown as one combined SIS course column but draw evidence from both College Board course frameworks.
Evidence base
Official SIS High School Academics and Advanced Placement pages; official College Board AP Students course overviews and linked Course and Exam Description references.
Evidence date
Public pages reviewed on 2 September 2026.
Unit of analysis
The crosswalk assesses similarity in named cognitive practice, not content overlap, exam difficulty, instructional quality, or student performance.
Local refinement
Departments can raise or lower a score after reviewing their current SIS syllabus, assessments, AP Audit materials, and classroom evidence.

K–5 Crosswalk Guide

All 20 habits remain available. Highlighted developmental priorities show where SIS places additional modeling and practice in K–2 and Grades 3–5.

Domain I · Habit 1

Prior Knowledge Activating

K–2 priority
K–2 expression
Recall a familiar experience, image, word, or earlier lesson and connect it to the new learning.
Grades 3–5 expression
Retrieve relevant prior learning, distinguish useful from distracting knowledge, and use it to frame the new task.
Strongest Domain 3 links
3a Purpose & Content; 3e Flexible Response
Strongest Domain 4 links
No defining link; see supporting scores
Principal look-for
Students connect the current task to earlier learning; the teacher surfaces and responds to inaccurate prior ideas.
Coaching question
What prior knowledge must students retrieve, and how will you learn whether it is accurate?
Domain I · Habit 2

Meaning-Making

K–2 priority
K–2 expression
Explain in simple language why an idea matters or where it appears in a familiar context.
Grades 3–5 expression
Explain the significance or usefulness of an idea and relate details to a larger concept or purpose.
Strongest Domain 3 links
3a Purpose & Content; 3c Student Engagement
Strongest Domain 4 links
No defining link; see supporting scores
Principal look-for
Students explain why an idea matters, what it helps them understand, or where it can be used.
Coaching question
Where will students make meaning rather than only receive or complete information?
Domain I · Habit 3

Purposeful Attending

K–2 priority
K–2 expression
Attend to one important feature, restate the focus, and filter out nonessential details with support.
Grades 3–5 expression
Identify the information, representation, or step that deserves attention and explain why it matters.
Strongest Domain 3 links
3a Purpose & Content; 3c Student Engagement
Strongest Domain 4 links
No defining link; see supporting scores
Principal look-for
Students can name the learning focus and direct attention to the features that carry the concept.
Coaching question

Domain I · Habit 4

Productive Struggling

Grades 3–5 priority
